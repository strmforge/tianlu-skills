# Small-Business Filing Calendar Readiness Review

Status: incubating.

## Signal

External feeds surface a recurring back-office workflow around annual reports, Statements of Information, franchise-tax or tax-calendar reminders, sales-tax filing frequency, BOI rule confusion, and business-license renewal. The useful solo-operator mechanism is not "keep a company compliant," "file taxes correctly," or "guarantee deadline safety." It is a preparation workflow: turn official agency pages, filing schedules, notices, and owner-supplied entity facts into an official-source status table, entity and jurisdiction map, filing deadline ledger, issue queue, reviewer queue, and no-file/no-payment handoff.

In the current scan, Chrome/X results were sparse, noisy, and often weakly related. `annual report filing small business` and `sales tax filing reminder small business` mostly returned generic tax or credit chatter. `business license renewal small business` and `Statement of Information LLC` were also weak and off-target. `BOI filing small business` surfaced some useful rule-confusion language around the March 26, 2025 BOI changes, but even there the main value was search language and pain language, not demand proof. That shape matters. It suggests a real operations lane, but one that lives in administrative calendars and official portals rather than in a hype-heavy social feed or open-source automation niche.

## Source

- FinCEN BOI reporting page: updated March 26, 2025; all entities created in the United States, including entities previously treated as domestic reporting companies, and their beneficial owners are now exempt from BOI reporting requirements; existing foreign companies still have BOI obligations under the revised rule and deadlines.
- IRS Small Business Tax Calendar: the IRS maintains a quarterly business tax calendar for key deadlines and actions; taxpayers are directed to use the calendar to avoid missing important deadlines; EFTPS timing and disaster-related extensions are explicit reminders that timing and current form guidance matter.
- Indiana INBiz business entity report page: business-entity reports are a recurring state-filing example and show that entity-report timing, status, and renewal are specific to state workflows and official systems.
- California Secretary of State Statements of Information filing tips: failure to file required Statements of Information can trigger Franchise Tax Board penalties and suspension or forfeiture; subsequent statements follow a statutory filing window tied to the entity's formation month and entity type-specific schedule.
- New York State Tax Bulletin ST-275: registered sales-tax vendors must file returns; filing frequency depends on taxable activity and tax due; even no-activity periods still require filing by the due date; annual, quarterly, and part-quarterly or monthly filing structures are explicit.
- Washington Department of Revenue business-license renewal page: license renewal and update flows are distinct from tax filing and business registration facts; renewal lives inside an official account and payment surface even when the public guidance page is readable.
- Community and GitHub search samples: useful only as weak ecosystem and search-language evidence. They do not prove demand, filing accuracy, legal status, compliance status, deadline safety, or repeatable solo-operator revenue.

## Proposed Trigger

Use this candidate when a founder, solo operator, bookkeeper, operations assistant, local-business helper, filing-service assistant, startup back-office generalist, or small-business owner needs a small-business filing-calendar readiness packet, annual-report deadline map, Statement of Information schedule review, BOI current-rule status table, sales-tax filing-frequency review, business-license renewal queue, official-source stale-link check, filing portal readback summary, multi-jurisdiction filing ledger, or no-file/no-payment/no-contact handoff.

Use it especially when the task is preparation, status mapping, and owner handoff before actual filing or payment. Do not use it as filing authority, legal or tax advice, compliance certification, right-to-operate determination, deadline guarantee, agency-contact workflow, portal-write workflow, payment workflow, or entity-status decision.

## Proposed Mechanism

0. Classify evidence strength before framing the opportunity.
   - L0: X post, reminder post, scare post, consultant thread, tax-tip post, community comment, or generic filing-anxiety post. Use only as pain language and search language.
   - L1: consultant pages, reminder tools, bookkeeping blogs, local business-help pages, or unofficial filing explainer pages. Treat as workflow-shape evidence, not filing authority or deadline proof.
   - L2-lite: official agency pages, official tax-calendar pages, official Secretary of State pages, official sales-tax bulletins, official license-renewal pages, official BOI alerts, official fee schedules, and official portal readbacks. Treat as process and terminology authority, not entity-specific filing completion authority.
   - L2-strong: owner-supplied formation date, entity type, state registration facts, filing notices, portal screenshots or readbacks, payment drafts, prior filings, tax-account notices, or scoped engagement evidence.
1. Define the filing-calendar boundary.
   - Name entity, jurisdiction, agency, filing lane, filing period, source snapshot, allowed artifact, and excluded actions.
   - Separate preparation from filing, submitting, paying, amending, correcting, certifying, contacting agencies, changing portal/account data, and writing to a spreadsheet or system of record.
2. Inventory official and jurisdiction-specific sources.
   - Capture annual-report pages, Statement of Information schedules, BOI rule alerts, IRS calendar pages, state sales-tax filing rules, business-license renewal guidance, portal requirements, fee schedules, and stale or broken source risks.
   - Record URL or file name, source date, jurisdiction, agency, source role, deadline type, stale-source risk, and whether the source is official, owner-supplied, login-gated, or L0/L1 signal.
3. Classify the filing pathway.
   - Separate annual report, Statement of Information, franchise-tax or tax-calendar reminder, sales-tax filing frequency, BOI current-rule review, business-license renewal, permit renewal, entity-status lookup, and no-activity filing path.
   - Use statuses such as `source-supported candidate`, `needs entity facts`, `needs jurisdiction split`, `needs portal readback`, `needs owner review`, `needs accountant review`, `needs legal review`, `stale`, `broken`, `login-gated`, `conflict`, and `ready for owner handoff`.
4. Build the filing deadline ledger.
   - For each filing lane, record entity, jurisdiction, agency, form or filing type, deadline logic, filing frequency, filing window, portal, payment surface, fee note, no-activity rule, missing evidence, conflicting source, stale source, reviewer role, and forbidden next action.
   - Keep tax IDs, owner identity data, payment details, account credentials, and sensitive registration records as sensitive. Prefer pointers, redacted summaries, or document labels unless scoped retention is explicitly approved.
5. Prepare the reviewer queue.
   - Turn gaps into questions for the owner, bookkeeper, accountant, tax preparer, lawyer, compliance reviewer, registered agent, licensing office reviewer, or state filing help surface.
   - Do not contact any agency, tax office, Secretary of State office, Department of Revenue office, registered agent, or third party without scoped approval.
6. Review account, filing, and payment boundaries.
   - Mark portal login, form completion, filing submission, annual-report certification, Statement of Information submission, BOI report filing, tax-return filing, sales-tax web filing, business-license renewal, payment authorization, status-check action, and agency email or phone contact as owner-approved follow-up only.
   - Treat filing portals, tax accounts, business-license accounts, and payment surfaces as account or action boundaries, not read-write permission.
7. Package the owner handoff.
   - Provide official-source status table, entity and jurisdiction map, filing deadline ledger, BOI current-rule status table, stale or broken source queue, reviewer queue, open questions, rollback or correction-review plan, and no-file/no-submit/no-payment/no-agency-contact/no-portal-write/no-system-write receipt.
   - Route executable actions to operations handoff only after explicit scoped approval.

## Initial Scope

This candidate is for source mapping, evidence organization, conservative draft support, deadline-ledger creation, reviewer handoff, and owner decision support. It must not file annual reports, Statements of Information, BOI reports, sales-tax returns, franchise-tax returns, or business-license renewals; make payments; create or access filing accounts without scoped approval; update systems of record; contact agencies; decide legal or tax obligations; guarantee compliance or deadline safety; decide right-to-operate status; or retain sensitive business-owner, tax, payment, or account records without scoped approval.

It should produce an official-source status table, entity and jurisdiction map, filing deadline ledger, BOI current-rule status table, annual-report or Statement-of-Information or sales-tax or business-license queue, stale or broken source queue, reviewer queue, open questions, risk boundary, and no-action receipt.

## Known Failure Modes

- Treating reminder pages or public agency pages as proof that a specific business is fully compliant or safe.
- Repeating stale BOI language that assumes all domestic U.S. entities still must file.
- Collapsing annual reports, Statements of Information, franchise tax, sales tax, BOI, and business-license renewals into one generic checklist.
- Applying one state or agency schedule to another state, entity type, or filing lane.
- Treating a calendar reminder as permission to file, pay, certify, amend, or contact an agency.
- Missing no-activity filing rules, filing-frequency shifts, filing windows, fee changes, or login-gated portal boundaries.
- Logging into filing portals, submitting forms, making payments, changing entity records, or contacting agencies without scoped approval.
- Treating X posts, consultant reminders, or GitHub projects as proof of demand, correctness, or filing authority.
- Retaining excessive tax, payment, identity, address, account, or filing-history data.
